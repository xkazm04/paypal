# Proof sheet

## The bet
The page answers with six claims, each graded by who stands behind it, such as "Every price here is signed" or "Dan says $329.00 was captured" (seller-attested). The claim that matters most is selected on arrival, its proof open in the inspector.

## How a person uses it
1. Layer 1 shows the amount, state and strip, one decision row with its countdown and default on silence, a two-line "What happened", and six claim rows with their grades.
2. J and K, or 1 to 6, move the selection. The inspector (layer 2) follows it with the proof. Enter opens the proof as a full sheet.
3. V re-verifies every signature and hash link offline, and the rows tick as it goes.
4. A opens the approval window. W opens the withdraw sheet, and a second W sends it. E exports the signed transcript.
5. G or Ctrl K jumps to a deal, the bracket keys step through deals, and Keys lists them all. Esc closes the top layer, then goes back.

## What it does better than the other two
It grades the evidence instead of listing it. A seller-attested claim reads differently from a receipted one, and unknown stays unknown. Every action has a key.

## Known limits
- The inspector is narrow, so the full envelope table needs the sheet.
- The narrative is templated per state.
- The approval window and the PayPal events are simulated.
