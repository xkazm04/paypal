# Replay

## The bet
A deal is a sequence of signed moments, so time leads the page. For a haggle, a chart shows bids and asks converging against the market band and the clause-4 ceiling. Other kinds get swim-lanes. A playhead rebuilds the deal as of any step, and the past can be inspected but never acted on.

## How a person uses it
1. Pick a deal from the filmstrip. The playhead starts at now, and the bottom dock holds the decision, its countdown and its default on silence.
2. Drag the playhead or use the arrow keys. Layer 1 shows the state strip as of that step and four indicators: the event, what PayPal knew, the mandate and counterparty, and the market.
3. Each indicator opens layer 2, either an event popover (signer, hash link, checks, untrusted note) or a sheet.
4. Replay plays the deal through to now. With reduced motion it jumps there.
5. At now the dock offers Review (the approval window) and Withdraw, which confirms in a sheet. In the past it offers only Jump to now and Export.

## What it does better than the other two
It shows how the agreement was reached and what the wallet knew at each step. Decisions happen only at now, so the past reads as evidence.

## Known limits
- Steps are evenly spaced, not to real time.
- Short deals give sparse lanes.
- The approval window and the PayPal events are simulated.
