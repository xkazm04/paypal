# Call script

## The bet
Make the compare a spoken ritual. Maya reads words 1 and 3; Dan reads 2 and 4 while her word stays hidden until she has heard his. Each word gets its own yes or no; the first "no" ends the pairing. Around it: one command field and a call log, keyboard-first.

## How a person uses it
1. Layer 1: type Dan's code, type HOUSE, or press N. A one-row "now" strip holds the step: the code with Copy/Cancel, or the four-turn strip with "Open approval window", plus an expiry meter and "silent →" line.
2. The approval window shows one turn: the word, Dan's reply, Y/N (Space reveals yours on his turns). A mismatch shows both words, one repeat, then abort (X).
3. After four matches, name the key and "Words match: pin key" (Enter). Idle-locked; abort is not.
4. The log is one line per event. Layer 2: spelling, expiry, HOUSE, fingerprint and "why turns" are popovers; "Details…" opens the pin's meaning, or why nothing was pinned, as a sheet.

## What it does better than the other two
Hardest to rubber-stamp: each word is its own act, and half are heard before seen.

## Known limits
- Slowest: four turns, two people on a call.
- Hiding Maya's word is a UX choice; the built window shows all four.
- HOUSE cannot speak, so it skips the turns and relies on its release pin.
- Dan's voice is simulated.
