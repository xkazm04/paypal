# The contract

## The bet
A mandate is a promise Maya makes about her agents, so it reads as one: a short contract in her words ("A single purchase may be at most **$200**, only for **office, parts or packing**"). Every bold term is a button. An edit redlines the sentence: old value struck, new in gold. She signs on the line at the foot.

## How a person uses it
1. Read M-12, S-2 or R-3. Layer 1 is the prose, a "this week" chip per clause, and the signature line.
2. Press a term. A popover (Layer 2) holds the control, the signed value and what this one change would have done. Enter keeps it; Esc undoes it.
3. Press a margin chip for that clause's deals, verdicts, reasons and market data.
4. Press "Sign v4…" on the line. The sheet holds the consequences, key, commitment and exact bytes. Unlock with Windows Hello (simulated), then sign; the signature draws in.
5. "Revoke" acts at once and stamps the document REVOKED. Past rows read as tracked changes.

## What it does better than the other two
Anyone can read it. The redline makes "what changes if I sign" obvious, and M-12 fits on one screen.

## Known limits
- S-2 (11 floors) still scrolls.
- Numeric edits are slower than in a form.
- It uses a prototype hash.
- Revoke passing the idle lock needs an owner decision.
