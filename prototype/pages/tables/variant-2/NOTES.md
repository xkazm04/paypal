# Gauge Board

## The bet
Everything visible, keyboard first. Every table is one row whose gauge puts the latest ask, your bid, the market band and your ceiling on one price line, so "how close" reads down a column. You edit rows in place; every edit collects in one mandate change you sign once.

## How a person uses it
1. Layer 1: a "Needs you" strip (countersign, offer lapses at 18:00 if silent) and the board. ↑ ↓ picks a row; Closeness reads "+$11 room" or "−$2 over".
2. ← → moves the ceiling, [ ] sets rounds, , . moves the deadline. Dragging the gauge, pips or track works too.
3. The bottom tray shows the diff as chips and flags a broken ask. S hands every edit to the approval window as one M-12 v4; Z undoes a row, Esc discards all.
4. Layer 2: I or ⓘ opens the row's sheet (prices, band, market, checks, clause 6, note). "What changes" holds the exact words; "Keys ?" holds the key map and legend.

## What it does better than the other two
It compares tables side by side, closed ones included, batches band moves into one signature, and is fastest from the keyboard.

## Known limits
- No time axis: history shows only as fading ticks.
- Each row has its own axis, so bar lengths don't compare.
- The key map sits behind "?".
- At 1024 px gauges are about 200 px wide.
