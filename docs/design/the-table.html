03 · Position

The spine: one Deal primitive, signed under one Mandate

All six capabilities are the same object at different tables. A Deal always has two sides and a closed set of terms. An Open Mandate is the owner's signed limit on what their agent may agree to. A Closed Mandate is the wallet's countersignature on one deal, and the wallet only issues it if that deal fits. PayPal is the settlement rail, not the policy engine: PayPal has no per-agent budgets S·paypal-platform §6.3.

Why the deal's terms are a closed list: one published shop-keeping agent experiment ended up with its agent telling customers to pay a hallucinated account. Its second run approved a contract nobody had meant to offer, and the fixes reported were a hard margin rule, procedures and role separation S·landscape §1.8. A closed list of deal types is the research note's own inference from those lessons (landscape §1.9 is marked [inference]), adopted here as a design choice A. Here, an offer is (item_ref, price, currency, delivery) and nothing else. The open/closed mandate split mirrors AP2 v0.2's open stage (constraints + agent public key, user-signed) and closed stage (bound to one transaction) S·landscape §1.3. The mandate is committed to the counterparty as a hash, so your ceiling stays private but can be proven later, in the spirit of AP2's selective disclosure (SD-JWT) S·landscape §1.3 A: commitment scheme.

06 · Machine · centerpiece

The haggle protocol: two wallets at one table

Two installs, two sandbox accounts, one agent each S·scope §3. The agents exchange signed envelopes through a store-and-forward rendezvous that can't read keys, change terms or touch PayPal. Each agent can only name a number, and the Rust core signs it only if it falls inside that owner's band. Settlement is one order that B issues and A approves.

The model may pick any number it likes. The wallet will only sign numbers inside the owner's band, and an unsigned offer doesn't exist.How limits bind each agent

fn send_offer(&self, d: &Deal, price: Money) -> Result<Envelope, Refusal> {
  let m = self.mandates.active_for(d)?;          // clause 1, 2
  m.band(d.item_ref())?.contains(price, d.side)?;  // clause 4
  m.rounds_left(d)?.at_least(1)?;                  // clause 4
  m.before_deadline(now())?;                       // clause 4
  self.velocity.check(&m, price)?;                 // clause 5
  let env = Envelope::offer(d, price, self.next_seq(d));
  self.ledger.append_out(&env)?;                   // hash chain first
  Ok(self.signer.sign_agent(&m.agent_key, env))   // key never in JS/LLM
}
// accept_offer: same checks against THEIR last signed price,
// plus clause 6 (HumanPresentOver) → queue a countersign card.

6.5 Settlement: a seller-issued order

1 · Both sign ACCEPTThe deal is closed with two signatures. Clause 6 may require A's owner to click countersign.
2 · B creates the orderPOST /v2/checkout/orders with intent: AUTHORIZE from B's own REST app (B is the merchant). The amount is exactly the deal amount S·paypal-platform §1.1, §2
3 · Link in a signed SETTLEThe approve link (rel: payer-action S·landscape §3.5) travels to A inside B's signed envelope.
4 · A approvesA's wallet checks the link against the signed deal, then opens it in the system browser S·landscape §3.5. The owner approves as buyer.
5 · B authorizes, captures, signsB polls GET /v2/checkout/orders/{id} until APPROVED, calls …/{id}/authorize, then /v2/payments/authorizations/{id}/capture on delivery, and sends a RECEIPT with the capture id S·paypal-platform §1.1, §1.2, §4
  • Always AUTHORIZE, as the research recommends. The note's framing for haggling is "an AUTHORIZE order or invoice that B issues and A's human approves" S·paypal-platform §2. Round 1 used CAPTURE for digital goods; that is withdrawn. The Delivery enum now decides only when B captures: DigitalNow straight after authorize, ShipThenCapture inside the 3-day honor period S·paypal-platform §1.2. Until capture, a hold exists and B can void it if the deal falls through. Calling /orders/{id}/capture on an AUTHORIZE order returns 422 ACTION_DOES_NOT_MATCH_INTENT S·paypal-platform §1.1.
  • invoice_id = {deal ULID}-{attempt}, never the display label. PayPal treats invoice_id as unique per merchant and rejects duplicates with DUPLICATE_INVOICE_ID S·paypal-platform §1.1, so B can't settle the same deal twice. The house seller is one merchant shared by every judge, so a per-install label like "D-0193" would collide across installs; a ULID can't A. The attempt suffix exists because an expired order may still hold its invoice_id A: unverified, spike.
  • The deal hash travels with the order. custom_id = first 32 hex characters of terms_hash and description = "Deal 01JD…7Q · monitor-27-4k". The field semantics and length limits are R and must be checked against the spec on day 1.
  • The approval window is shown as a countdown. The link has 6 hours by default S·paypal-platform §1.1. If it expires, B re-issues the order with attempt+1 (at most 3).
  • Never Friends & Family. Goods and services must be paid as a commercial transaction S·paypal-platform §5. The scam shield flags any NOTE asking for "personal" payment as the User Agreement's "Buyer Scam" typology S·paypal-platform §5.

5 · Against the other shortlisted variant (A-1 Checkpoint): why build The Table

The Table keeps A-1's guarantee

A mandate clause is a gate rule. A request outside the mandate is refused before any PayPal call, with the clause named and zero rows in paypal_calls (test F1), which is exactly A-1's Deny path. The approval lives in a separate window label, as in A-1 (#c1). Choosing The Table loses none of A-1's safety.

…and adds the counterparty

Three of the six capabilities have someone on the other side (haggle, shop, rescue). In A-1 they are sources of intents feeding one lane (#spine). Here the other side is on screen: who they are, whether they're paired, what the market says, and what they signed. That is the "practical business situation anyone can understand" the owner asked for: Maya buys a monitor from Dan for less than the market median, and both hold the proof.

The prize bet is less contested

"Best Use of Agentic Commerce" is agent-to-agent commerce, which is this report's literal subject. Channel3 is one $1,500 prize that few entries will call meaningfully A, and here it is load-bearing (the band). A-1 aims at AG Grid, the largest sponsor pot. Its own prize section flags a floor case of an HM plus an AG Grid runner-up (#prize).

Its haggle settles on CAPTURE, where the research recommends AUTHORIZE or an invoice (#c9).
