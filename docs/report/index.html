The rule every wave kept

Money moves at PayPal only from the Rust pipeline, and only on one of three authorities. Each authority is recorded per money step in decided_by and in the hash-chained audit log.

1 · The owner, in the approval window

A hold-to-confirm decision in the one window whose commands can countersign, release or approve. It needs a token, the right window label and an unlocked wallet. Since T5 it is bound to the exact checklist the owner saw.

2 · A rule the owner signed

A countersign under the owner's threshold, or the seller collecting an order the buyer already approved on PayPal. The house seller acts on its release-pinned rules.

3 · A safe default

Void the hold or let it lapse at the deadline. Never capture. Silence never moves money, and since wave 9 the wallet records what the owner was shown before a deadline default applied.

The agent has no tool that pays, captures, voids or refunds. A refused intent leaves zero PayPal calls. The gauntlet re-checks this across the whole ledger after every generated session.

What a merchant gets

The same work, grouped by the product goals: automate the business, handle payments, protect, and stay understandable.

Automate the business

  • Tables: agents haggle inside a signed price range, including a deterministic negotiator that needs no LLM. Every agreed price carries a re-checkable fair-price certificate.
  • Shop around: one item, several sellers, first agreement wins.
  • Counter: the owner's own shop. Buyers' agents order there, never below the lowest prices, and buyer-approved orders are collected without a click.
  • Rescue: watched subscriptions, one discount fix per failed renewal.
  • MarketWatch: fresh market prices under a signed allowance.

Payments that add up

  • Holds at PayPal, then capture or release. The read-back resolver makes each money step exactly-once.
  • Wallet-wide limits above every mandate.
  • Book: every payment in one list, matched to PayPal's statement, with "ask in your own words".
  • Amounts agree across every surface (polish 3).

Protect

  • Shield: new payees and odd prices pause before PayPal is asked. Only the owner can release.
  • Rust-attested checklist bound to each decision.
  • Proof bundle v2 plus an offline verifier anyone can run.
  • Gauntlet and acceptance matrix in CI; a safety dossier artifact; Your safety record in the app.
  • Authority manifest: one table decides who may call each command.

Understandable, world-class UX

  • Rewind: who decided each money step.
  • Walk-away forecast and What you were shown: silence is explained, never feared.
  • While you were away, first run, WCAG 2.2 AA, three polish passes.
  • Maya's week director: judges watch it work without installing anything.

Honest edges

Done and verified

  • Two adversarial reviews of the money paths found no broken invariant. Every finding was fixed with a regression test.
  • Flaky tests root-caused, never skipped: actor gated polls, and one-tick states.
  • CI red for ~6 hours (Windows file-name collisions, CRLF prompts) was found and fixed, and a guard was added.
  • Dev builds now optimise crypto and SQLite, which makes the gauntlet about 5× faster.

Needs the owner

  • Rescue double charge: after a fix's invoice is paid, PayPal may still retry the full renewal. Stopping it (suspend, or charging the owed balance instead of invoicing) is a lever-design decision.
  • Spike 8: one live sandbox invoice and subscription read. Several subscription fields are marked UNVERIFIED.
  • T3 real engine session stays gated on spike 2. The deterministic negotiator carries the demo.
  • Native Windows UAT: notifications, Do Not Disturb, Hello. Acceptance IDs not yet covered by Rust tests: F5, M1, M2, C2, R2, U1–U4, W1, W2, W7, W9.
